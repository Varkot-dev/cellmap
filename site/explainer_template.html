cellmap

Do deep models describe cells better than PCA?

I compared a 1901 statistics method with a variational autoencoder and a transformer I wrote from scratch, and with three published foundation models, on blood cells from people. The simple methods held up.

Scroll to see how, and why. You can drag the map to rotate it.

1 / The data

Every dot is one blood cell

Single-cell RNA sequencing counts, in each cell, the RNA molecules from each of about 20,000 genes. That makes each cell a list of 20,000 numbers, mostly zero.

To compare cells, every method squeezes that list into a short vector, an embedding. A good embedding puts cells of the same type together. This map shows one, drawn in 3D.

2 / The baseline

Plain PCA already sorts cells by type

PCA finds the directions in which cells vary most and keeps the top 50. Here it is fitted on 2,000 informative genes, and the colors are the cell types the original study assigned.

T cells, B cells, monocytes and NK cells each form their own regions, with no labels used to make the map.

3 / The problem

Color the same map by lab chemistry, and it splits in two

Two of the 21 donors were sequenced with a newer kit. Their cells form separate islands, even for the same cell type.

This is a batch effect: a technical difference that looks like biology. Correcting it, without erasing real differences, is the hard part of the field.

4 / A fix

Harmony pulls the two kits together

Harmony groups cells into soft clusters and nudges each donor's cells toward the shared center of each cluster. It was told only which person each cell came from, never the chemistry or the cell type.

The islands merge.

5 / At a cost

Cell types survive, but fine detail blurs

Harmony scored higher on the standard integration benchmark in every test. It also lost fine-grained accuracy in every test: close subtypes, such as naive and memory T cells, got harder to tell apart.

So "best method" depends on what you measure. Every result here reports both.

6 / A fair test

Whole people are held out

Cells from one person share genetics and handling. If the same person sits in training and test, a model can score well by recognizing the person.

So every method is fitted on 13 people and scored on 4 it has never seen, and this is repeated with 5 different groups of held-out people. Orange cells are the held-out people in one of those splits.

7 / Deep model one

A variational autoencoder, written by hand

An encoder squeezes each cell into 10 numbers; a decoder, told which person the cell came from, rebuilds the raw counts with a negative binomial likelihood. Telling the decoder the person is what removes donor effects from the code.

It matched the reference library, scvi-tools, within noise. It packs as much cell-type information into 10 numbers as PCA does into 50, but it did not beat PCA or Harmony.

8 / Deep model two

A transformer, also written by hand

Each cell becomes a set of tokens, one per active gene, with its expression level. The model learns by hiding some levels and predicting them, like a language model filling in words. Trained on a GPU.

It learned that task, but its map is the weakest of the project's own models: macro F1 against for PCA.

9 / Foundation models

Models pretrained on millions of cells

This is TranscriptFormer, pretrained on 57 million cells. The study also scores Census scVI and a Geneformer fine-tuned with cell-type labels.

All three had seen these exact cells during training, an advantage the other methods did not get. None beat plain PCA.

The results

The rule was written down before any deep model was scored: a method is "better" only if it beats both HVG + PCA and PCA + Harmony in at least 4 of the 5 held-out-donor splits. of methods passed it on either measure below. Each dot is one split; the bar is the mean.

Telling cell types apart

Mixing people while keeping types

Simple is strong

PCA, a method from 1901, was never clearly beaten at telling cell types apart. Harmony on top of it won the integration score.

Metrics disagree

The method that mixes people best is not the one that separates close cell types best. A single leaderboard number would hide that.

Scale did not rescue it

Pretrained models that had seen these cells, and a transformer trained from scratch, both fell short of PCA here.
